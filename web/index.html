A market you can break

Simulated traders meet in a limit order book with price-time priority. Market makers quote both sides, noise traders trade at random, momentum traders chase moves, and informed traders know the hidden fair value. Change who is trading, cause a flash crash, or trade yourself. The matching rules are a JavaScript port of the C++ engine in this repo, which handles about 31 million orders a second.

Things to try

  • Remove the market makers. The spread blows out, the book often goes one-sided, and the price wanders far from fair value. Liquidity is what makes prices informative.
  • Remove the informed traders. Nothing ties the price to fair value any more, so it drifts with noise and momentum.
  • Turn momentum traders up to 10, then press Flash crash. Trend followers pile in behind the sell program, the makers widen their quotes, and the drop gets much deeper.
  • Watch the agent table. Informed traders make money and noise traders pay for it. Market makers earn the spread in calm markets but get run over when the fair value jumps (adverse selection).
  • Buy 2,000 shares at market. You walk the book and pay more on each level. The gap between the price you paid and the mid is your market impact.

The C++ engine

The engine is a single-instrument limit order book written in C++20 with no dependencies:

  • Price levels sit in a flat array indexed by tick, so finding a level is one memory access.
  • A 3-layer hierarchical bitset finds the next best price after a level empties, using at most 3 word scans for 262,144 ticks.
  • Orders are pooled and linked into a per-level FIFO, so cancels are O(1) with no allocation once warm.
  • It supports limit, market, IOC and FOK orders, cancels, and amends (a smaller size keeps time priority; a larger one loses it).
  • A randomised test drives it and a simple std::map book with the same 400,000 operations and requires identical trades and depth after every step.
